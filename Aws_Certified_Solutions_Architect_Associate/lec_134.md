## 1️⃣ What Is Data? 📊

> 💡 **Core Idea:** Data is simply **raw facts**: raw numbers, raw figures, raw information.

```
🔢 Numbers in a spreadsheet
✉️ Text in an email
📷 Images stored on your phone
```

> 🧒 **In simple words:** Data is the **raw ingredients** in your kitchen: flour, eggs, sugar. By themselves they're just items. They only become a cake once you do something useful with them.

> 📈 The amount of data being created is **growing exponentially every year**, and it comes in **many different forms**.

---

## 2️⃣ The Three Types of Data 🗂️

| Type | What It Looks Like | Examples | 🧒 Simple Picture |
|---|---|---|---|
| 📋 **Structured** | **Neat tables** with rows and columns, organized in **relational databases** | Customer table, sales records | A **well-organized spreadsheet** |
| 🧩 **Semi-structured** | Has **some structure but very flexible**; formats like **JSON** or **XML** | API responses, config files | A **form where you can add extra notes** in the margin |
| 🖼️ **Unstructured** | **No standard structure** | Photos, videos, archive files | A **box of mixed photos and papers** |

> 🎯 **Key Insight:** Different data types need **different storage and handling**. We'll see why in the coming lectures.

## 3️⃣ From Data to Intelligence 💡

> 💡 **Core Idea:** **Intelligence** means taking data and turning it into **actionable insights**, so you can **make better decisions**.

```
📊 DATA         = what you HAVE
💡 INTELLIGENCE = what the data MEANS and what you should DO about it
```

### 🎓 Example 1: The Weather 🍦

| | |
|---|---|
| 📊 **Data** | "It is 30° outside." |
| 💡 **Intelligence** | "Maybe I should eat an ice cream!" |

> 😄 The instructor jokes that in Hyderabad, if he ate ice cream every time it hit 30°C, he'd be eating it **more than nine months a year**, which wouldn't be great for his health! Even a simple example shows that intelligence is about **useful action**, not just reacting to a number.

> 📝 *Note: The lecture says "30°F" at first and then "30°C." The Hyderabad joke only makes sense in Celsius, so the first one was most likely a slip.*

### 🎓 Example 2: The Cancelled Subscription 🔄

| | |
|---|---|
| 📊 **Data** | "Customer X cancelled their subscription." |
| 💡 **Intelligence** | "They cancelled because of **price**. Let's offer a **discount** to keep them." |

## 4️⃣ How Do We Get Intelligence? 🔧

> 💡 **The Answer:** Through **Analytics** and **Artificial Intelligence (AI)**.

| Approach | Time Focus | Main Job |
|---|---|---|
| 📈 **Analytics** | **The PAST** (and present) | Understand **what happened** and **why** |
| 🤖 **AI / Machine Learning** | **The FUTURE** | **Predict** what will happen and **recommend** what to do |

---

## 5️⃣ The Four Questions of Analytics 🪜

> 🚗 **Running Example:** A car's engine.

| # | Type | Question It Answers | Engine Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1️⃣ | 📋 **Descriptive** | **What happened?** | "The engine is **overheating**." |
| 2️⃣ | 🔍 **Diagnostic** | **Why did it happen?** | "Because the **coolant tank is empty**." |
| 3️⃣ | 🔮 **Predictive** | **What will happen?** | "If you drive **10 more miles**, the engine **will fail**." |
| 4️⃣ | 🧭 **Prescriptive** | **What should we do?** | "**Stop now**, refill the coolant, and don't drive further." |

### 🧒 Think of a Doctor 🩺

```
📋 Descriptive   → "You have a fever."
🔍 Diagnostic    → "It's because of an infection."
🔮 Predictive    → "If untreated, it will get worse in two days."
🧭 Prescriptive  → "Take this medicine and rest."
```

### 🎯 How They Group

```
📈 ANALYTICS  (looks at the PAST)   → Descriptive + Diagnostic
🤖 AI / ML    (looks at the FUTURE) → Predictive + Prescriptive
```

## ❓ Extra Important Question: "Are the four types of analytics separate tools, or do they build on each other?"

> 💡 **Answer:** They **build on each other**, like steps on a ladder. You can't explain *why* something happened (diagnostic) until you know *what* happened (descriptive). You can't predict *what's coming* (predictive) until you understand the patterns from the past. And you can't recommend the *best action* (prescriptive) until you can predict the outcomes of different choices. As you climb the ladder, it's commonly described that each step gives **more value** but is also **harder to do**. Most businesses start with descriptive reporting, and gradually move up. This is also why **good, well-stored data** matters so much: every step on the ladder depends on having reliable data to work with.
