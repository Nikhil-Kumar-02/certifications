## 1️⃣ Object-Level Logging with CloudTrail 🔍

> 💡 **Core Idea:** You can log **all the API calls** made to your bucket by using **AWS CloudTrail**.

```
Bucket → Properties → Object-level logging
   → create a trail in CloudTrail and associate it with your S3 bucket
```

### 📌 What Gets Tracked?

```
👁️ READ events  (like downloading or reading an object)
✍️ WRITE events (like uploading or deleting an object)
```

> 🎯 **Exam Tip:** **All read and write events** on an S3 bucket can be tracked with **CloudTrail**.

> 🧒 **In simple words:** **CloudTrail** is AWS's **security camera and diary**. It records **who did what, and when**. Turning on object-level logging points that camera at your bucket, so every read, upload and delete gets written down. 📹

---

## 2️⃣ Why Encrypt Data? 🔐

> 💡 **Core Idea:** The best practice is to **always encrypt data when you store it in the cloud.**

> 🧒 **In simple words:** Encryption is like **putting your documents in a locked box before storing them**. Even if someone got hold of the box, they couldn't read what's inside without the **key**. 🗝️

---

## 3️⃣ The Encryption Options in S3 🔑

Navigate: **Bucket → Properties → Default encryption**

| Option | Name | Who Manages the Keys? |
|---|---|---|
| 🚫 **None** | No encryption (the default shown in the lecture) | N/A |
| 🔐 **AES-256** | **SSE-S3**: *Server-Side Encryption with S3-Managed keys* | **AWS/S3** manages the keys and encrypts objects automatically |
| 🗝️ **AWS-KMS** | **SSE-KMS**: server-side encryption with keys from **KMS** | **You control the keys** through **AWS Key Management Service (KMS)** |

> 📝 **"Server-side" means:** S3 **does the encrypting for you** after receiving your file. You don't have to encrypt it before uploading.

### 🗝️ A Closer Look at the KMS Option

```
You can pick:
   🔑 The built-in key called  aws/s3  (an AWS-managed key), OR
   🔑 Your OWN key, created in KMS
```

### 🧒 In Simple Words: Who Holds the Key? 🏨

| Option | 🧒 Picture |
|---|---|
| 🔐 **SSE-S3** | A **hotel safe** where the **hotel manages the key**. Easy, no setup |
| 🗝️ **SSE-KMS** | A **safe with your own key** that you control and can **track who used it**. More control, a bit more effort |

> 📚 **More to come:** The lecture says **KMS and its S3 integration** will be covered **in depth in the KMS section** later. For now, just remember the **three options**.

### 🆕 Modern Note

> 📝 **Since the lecture was recorded, AWS changed the default:** **all new objects uploaded to S3 are now encrypted automatically with SSE-S3**, even if you do nothing. So today, "no encryption" is no longer the default. You can still **choose SSE-KMS** if you want control over the keys.

## 4️⃣ Forcing Encryption with a Policy 🛡️

> 💡 **Core Idea:** You can set up a policy on the bucket that says:
>
> **"Reject any `PUT` (upload) request that does not use encryption."**

```
📤 Upload with encryption    → ✅ Accepted
📤 Upload without encryption → ❌ Rejected
```

> 🧒 **In simple words:** It's a **front-desk rule**: *"We only accept parcels that arrive in a locked box. Unlocked parcels are turned away at the door."*

> 🔗 **Recall Lecture 147:** This kind of rule is written in a **bucket policy**, a resource-based policy.

## ❓ Extra Important Question: "Server access logging (Lecture 145) and CloudTrail both record requests to my bucket. What's the difference, and which should I use?"

> 💡 **Answer:** They overlap, but they're built for **different needs**:
>
> | | 📜 **Server Access Logging** | 🔍 **CloudTrail (object-level)** |
> |---|---|---|
> | 🎯 **Purpose** | Detailed **access records** for **usage analysis** and basic auditing | **Auditing and security**: *who* made *which API call*, *when* |
> | 📦 **Where logs go** | Log files delivered to an **S3 bucket** | A **trail** that can deliver to **S3** and **CloudWatch** |
> | ⏱️ **Delivery** | **Best-effort**, can take **hours** (as we saw) | Typically **much faster** |
> | 🔗 **Integration** | Standalone | Works with **other AWS services**, alerts, and a single place to audit **all** AWS activity |
> | 💰 **Cost** | No extra charge for the logging itself (you pay for storing the log files) | **Object-level (data) events are charged** |
>
> 🧒 **In simple words:** Server access logging is the **visitor register at the front desk**. CloudTrail is the **building's security system** that watches **every door in the whole campus**. For **security auditing and compliance**, CloudTrail is usually the stronger choice. For **cheap usage analysis**, server access logs may be enough.
>
> *(Exact pricing and speeds can change, so check the current AWS docs before relying on them.)*
