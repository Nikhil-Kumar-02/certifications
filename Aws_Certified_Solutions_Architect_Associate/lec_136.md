## 🎯 What This Lecture Is About

> 🎬 Here's a question to start with: **Which is worse: your data being temporarily unavailable, or lost forever?** Most people say "lost forever", and this lecture shows why. Let's understand **availability** and **durability** in depth.

## 1️⃣ Availability 🚪

> 💡 **Core Idea:** Availability is about **access**. Is your data (or application) **available whenever it's needed**?

### 📏 How Is It Measured?

As an **uptime percentage** over a period of time (usually a month).

| Availability | Nickname | Allowed Downtime per Month* |
|---|---|---|
| 99% | Two nines | about 7 hours |
| 99.9% | Three nines | about 43 minutes |
| **99.95%** | | **about 22 minutes** *(the lecture's example)* |
| **99.99%** | **Four nines** | **about 4.5 minutes** *(strictly about 4.3)* |
| **99.999%** | **Five nines** | **about 26 seconds** |

> *Based on a 30-day month. The first two rows are extra, added for comparison.

### 🧒 In Simple Words

> 🏪 Think of a shop. **99.99% availability** means the shop is closed for only about **4 minutes in a whole month**. **99.999%** means closed for just **26 seconds**!

### 🎯 What's a Realistic Target?

| Target | Reality |
|---|---|
| ✅ **Four nines (99.99%)** | A common goal. Hard to achieve, but enough for most users |
| 😰 **Five nines (99.999%)** | **Very hard.** Your downtime allowance drops from about 4.5 minutes to just **26 seconds** a month, and in the real world, failures are common |

> 📝 *Note: The lecture says "four nines" is the usual target in one place and "99.9%" in another. Both are described as common targets, so treat 99.9%–99.99% as the typical range.*

---

## 2️⃣ How to Improve Availability 🛠️

| Technique | What It Means | 🧒 Simple Picture |
|---|---|---|
| 🔁 **Redundancy** | Keep **multiple instances or copies** of your app/data, so a **standby** is ready if one fails | Carrying a **spare tyre** |
| 🌍 **Geo-distribution** | Deploy across **multiple zones or regions**. If a whole data center or region fails, others keep serving users | A bank with **branches in different cities** |
| 🤖 **Automatic failover** | **Health checks** keep asking "Are you up?" The moment the main one fails, traffic **switches to the standby automatically** | A **backup generator** that switches on by itself when power cuts out |

> 🎯 **Key Insight:** Copies alone aren't enough. **Automation** makes the switch fast enough to matter. A standby that takes 30 minutes to start by hand won't help you reach four nines.

## 3️⃣ Durability 🗄️

> 💡 **Core Idea:** Durability is about **existence over time**. Will your data **still exist years from now**, even through multiple failures and disasters?

### 📏 How Is It Measured?

As the **probability of losing data** over a period of time.

```
🎯 Typical durability target: 11 NINES = 99.999999999%
```

> 🔎 *Reminder from Lecture 135: the lecture's "1 million files for 10 million years" figure is a bit off. The standard AWS wording is "10 million files, expect to lose about one every 10,000 years."*

### 🤔 Why Is the Durability Target So Much Higher Than Availability?

| Reason | Explanation |
|---|---|
| 💔 **Data loss is irreversible** | Once lost, it's generally **unrecoverable** |
| 📜 **Compliance** | Financial records, medical data and legal archives **must be kept**, and there may be rules about it |

> 🧒 **In simple words:** A shop being **closed for a few minutes** is annoying, but it reopens. A shop **burning down with all your belongings** is permanent. That's why we expect far more protection against the second.

---

## 4️⃣ How to Improve Durability 🛠️

| Technique | What It Means | 🧒 Simple Picture |
|---|---|---|
| 🔁 **Redundancy** | Store **multiple copies**, so if one is lost, others remain | **Photocopies** of an important document |
| 🌍 **Geo-distribution** | Keep copies in **separate zones and regions**, protecting against a data center failure or a regional disaster | Keeping copies at **home, at the office, and at a relative's house** |
| 📚 **Versioning** | Keep **every version** of a file whenever it changes. Even if the latest version is **accidentally deleted**, you can **go back** to an earlier one | The **"undo" or "version history"** in a document editor |

---

## 5️⃣ The Bank Analogy 🏧

| | Availability | Durability |
|---|---|---|
| 🏧 **In the bank story** | "Is the **ATM working right now**? Can I withdraw cash?" | "Is my **account balance record safe** at the bank?" |

### 🎭 The Worst Cases

```
🏧 ATM down for an hour      → Annoying, but temporary. You try again later.
📉 Bank loses your record    → Your money DISAPPEARS. This is the real disaster.
```

> 🎯 **Key Insight:** You can **survive** a temporary outage. You **cannot survive** permanent loss. That's why availability is always a challenge, but durability is usually the more critical one.

---

## 6️⃣ Availability vs Durability ⚖️

| Aspect | 🚪 Availability | 🗄️ Durability |
|---|---|---|
| 🎯 **Focus** | Is the data **accessible now**? | Is the data **never lost**? |
| 📏 **Typical goal** | **Four nines** (99.99%) | **Eleven nines** (99.999999999%) |
| 🛑 **Aims to avoid** | **Downtime** | **Permanent data loss** |
| 💥 **If it fails** | Temporary outage | **Permanent loss**, a much bigger impact (think stock or banking transactions) |
| 🛠️ **How to achieve** | Redundancy, geo-distribution, **failover** | Redundancy, geo-distribution, **versioning** |
| 📦 **Applies to** | **Both applications and data** (databases, file stores) | **Data stores** only (databases, data warehouses, file storage, object storage) |

---

## ❓ Extra Important Question: "Why is going from four nines to five nines so much harder than going from three nines to four?"

> 💡 **Answer:** Because each extra nine shrinks your **downtime allowance by 10×**, and eventually the allowance becomes smaller than the **time it takes to react**. At four nines you have about **4.5 minutes** a month. At five nines you have about **26 seconds**. A single failure that takes 1 minute to detect and recover from would **blow your whole month's budget** at five nines. So reaching it means **everything must be automatic and instant**: health checks, failover, redundant copies in several zones, with no human in the loop. Each extra nine also tends to cost **a lot more money and complexity**. That's why architects ask, *"What availability does this system actually need?"* instead of just aiming for the maximum.
